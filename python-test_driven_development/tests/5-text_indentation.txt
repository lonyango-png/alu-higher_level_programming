The ``5-text_indentation`` module
=================================

Using ``text_indentation``
--------------------------

Import the function from the module:

    >>> text_indentation = __import__('5-text_indentation').text_indentation

The function prints no new line at the end, so print() is added to the
examples that do not end with a delimiter.

Two new lines after a dot:

    >>> text_indentation("Hello. World"); print()
    Hello.
    <BLANKLINE>
    World

Two new lines after each of the characters . ? and :

    >>> text_indentation("A. B? C: D"); print()
    A.
    <BLANKLINE>
    B?
    <BLANKLINE>
    C:
    <BLANKLINE>
    D

A text that ends with a delimiter:

    >>> text_indentation("Hello.")
    Hello.
    <BLANKLINE>
    >>> text_indentation("Really?")
    Really?
    <BLANKLINE>
    >>> text_indentation("Note:")
    Note:
    <BLANKLINE>

A text without any delimiter:

    >>> text_indentation("Hello world"); print()
    Hello world
    >>> text_indentation("a"); print()
    a

Spaces after a delimiter are removed:

    >>> text_indentation("One.    Two"); print()
    One.
    <BLANKLINE>
    Two

Spaces at the beginning of the text are removed:

    >>> text_indentation("   Hello"); print()
    Hello

Delimiters that follow each other:

    >>> text_indentation("Wait... what?")
    Wait.
    <BLANKLINE>
    .
    <BLANKLINE>
    .
    <BLANKLINE>
    what?
    <BLANKLINE>

A delimiter in the middle of a word:

    >>> text_indentation("a.b"); print()
    a.
    <BLANKLINE>
    b

A text that already contains a new line:

    >>> text_indentation("Line1\nLine2"); print()
    Line1
    Line2

Empty text and text made of spaces print nothing:

    >>> text_indentation("")
    >>> text_indentation("    ")

The text must be a string:

    >>> text_indentation(5)
